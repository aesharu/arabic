# Saudi Arabic — the plan

A 15-month plan to talk with one person, in her dialect, about everyday life. Not movies, not news, not formal Arabic, not other countries' Arabic. **Modern everyday Saudi** — the way young Saudis talk, the way she talks.

**For Claude Code:** this file is the curriculum and the single source of truth for vocabulary. Instructions for what to build from it are in Part 9. Anything marked ⚠ is not fully verified — show it with a "check with tutor" flag and never present it as certain.

---

## Part 1 — What Saudi Arabic is, in one page

Saudis don't call their everyday speech "Najdi" — they call it **Saudi** (سعودي). What young Saudis speak today — at home, on Snapchat and TikTok, in series, in voice notes — is one shared, modern Saudi. It grew mostly out of the speech of Riyadh and the centre (Najd), everyone in the country understands it, and it leaves out the old village and Bedouin forms. **That's what this plan teaches, the way she speaks it** (she's from Hafar al-Batin, in the north-east).

Things that make it Saudi (and different from the Egyptian, Levantine, Gulf and formal Arabic that fill the internet and the textbooks):

| Feature | What it means for you |
|---|---|
| **ق is pronounced g** | قهوة = *gahwa* (coffee), قلبي = *galbi* (my heart), صديق = *ṣadīg* (friend). Only a few formal/religious words keep a real q. |
| **ض and ظ are the same sound** | Both are a heavy *dh* (as in "this", but heavier). ضيف = *ẓēf*. |
| **th and dh sounds are kept** | ثلاثة = *thalātha*, هذا = *hādha*. Egyptians turn these into t/s/d/z — don't copy them. |
| **ay → ē, aw → ō** | زين = *zēn*, يوم = *yōm*, وين = *wēn*. |
| **"your": -ik to her, -ak to him** | كيفك = *kēfik* to her, *kēfak* to him — the same letter ك, only the vowel changes. The -ich form (أحبچ) is Gulf — Kuwait, Iraq, the Emirates, Qatar, Bahrain — not Saudi (Dima, 22 Sept 2026). |
| **Verbs to a woman end in -īn** | تبي = *tibi* (you want, to him) → تبين = *tibīn* (to her). This is the ending you'll use most. |
| **Future = ب- in front of the verb** | أروح *arūḥ* (I go) → بروح *barūḥ* (I'll go). |
| **"What" = وش** | *wesh*. The most recognizable Saudi word (in Jeddah also إيش). |
| **"Very" = مرة, "not" = مو** | حلو مرة *ḥilw marra* — really nice; مو زين *mū zēn* — not good. Older Najdi says حيل *ḥēl* and مب *mub* — recognize them. |
| **Some sounds shift in old/rural speech** | ك can become *ts*, ج can become *y* in some families and regions. Recognize, don't copy. |

Sources for these points are listed in Part 10.

## Part 2 — How many words, honestly

- **~150 words + ~50 fixed phrases** → you can greet her, ask how she is, say what you're doing, and survive a voice note.
- **~500 words** → real daily conversation about your day, food, plans, feelings, family — slow, with gaps, but real.
- **~1000 words** → comfortable. You follow her normal-speed voice notes on familiar topics and tell stories about your week.

This plan gets you to 500 carefully chosen words in 8 months, then to 1000 by the end of 2027 — **and the second 500 come from her**, not from a list. Words she actually uses beat any frequency list ever made.

### The method in one sentence

Learn words **inside sentences**, speak **from week 3**, and let her voice notes be your main listening.

"Just start speaking" is right — but with memorized chunks, not by building sentences from grammar. You learn *wesh tsawwīn?* ("what are you doing?") as one piece, like a word. Grammar comes later to explain what you already say.

## Part 3 — The 15-month roadmap

At 1–2 hours a day. Dates assume a start of 21 September 2026.

| When | Stage | Words (total) | You can… |
|---|---|---|---|
| Weeks 1–2 · Sep 21 – Oct 4, 2026 | **Script** | — | Read any word slowly, write every letter in every form |
| Weeks 3–10 · Oct – Nov 2026 | **Stage 1: Core** | 150 + 50 phrases | Greet her, ask and answer "how are you", say what you're doing, say you don't understand |
| Months 3–5 · Dec 2026 – Feb 2027 | **Stage 2: Daily life** | ~320 | Talk about your day, food, family, what you'll do tomorrow — in short sentences |
| Months 5–8 · Feb – May 2027 | **Stage 3: Talking to her** | ~500 | A 10-minute call mostly in Arabic, with English as a rescue |
| Months 8–12 · Jun – Sep 2027 | **Stage 4: Her words** | ~750 | Tell a story about your week in the past tense; follow her voice notes on familiar topics |
| Months 12–15 · Oct – Dec 2027 | **Stage 5: Her words, deeper** | ~1000 | A 30-minute call in Arabic; joke, disagree, explain feelings simply |

**Ramadan 2027 falls roughly early February to early March** (approx. — check the exact dates nearer the time). That lands in Stage 2/3 and is a perfect moment to learn the Ramadan phrases in Part 7 and use them with her.

### A normal day (90 minutes)

| Block | Time | What |
|---|---|---|
| Cards | 20 min | Review + 3–5 new words, always as sentences with audio |
| Listening | 40 min | Her voice notes first. Then short Saudi vlogs/snaps if you want more. Half focused, half in the background. |
| Speaking | 30 min | Tutor session, or shadowing (repeat her voice notes out loud), or record a voice note back to her |

### A normal week

- **2 tutor sessions** (45 min, a Saudi tutor, speaking only) — these replace that day's speaking block
- **1 review day** on the weekend: print next week's sheets, write every new word by hand three times
- **Every day from week 3:** one small ritual with her in Arabic — a good-morning, a good-night, one question

### How she can help (without becoming your teacher)

- Ask for **voice notes, not text.** Spoken Saudi is barely written; text quietly teaches you formal spellings and hides pronunciation.
- Ask her for **one word a day** — any word she'd use. These become Stage 4–5.
- Ask her to correct **one thing per conversation**, not everything.
- The tutor handles grammar and drills. She's for real life.

## Part 5 — The grammar you actually need

Nine patterns. Learn them as examples, not rules.

### 5.1 Pronouns

| | Arabic | Say |
|---|---|---|
| I | أنا | ana |
| you (to a man) | انت | int |
| you (to a woman) | انتي | inti |
| he | هو | hu |
| she | هي | hi |
| we | حنا | ḥinna |
| you all | انتو | intu |
| they | هم | hum |

### 5.2 "My, your, her" — endings

| Ending | Meaning | Example |
|---|---|---|
| ـي -i | my | بيتي *bēti* — my house |
| ـك -ik | your (to her) | بيتك *bētik* — your house (to her) |
| ـك -ak | your (to him) | بيتك *bētak* — your house (to him) |
| ـه -ah / -ih | his | بيته *bētah* |
| ـها -ha | her | بيتها *bētha* |
| ـنا -na | our | بيتنا *bētna* |
| ـكم -kum | your (all) | بيتكم *bētkum* |
| ـهم -hum | their | بيتهم *bēthum* |
| ـني -ni | me (after a verb) | تحبني؟ *tḥibbīni?* — do you love me? |

### 5.3 "To be" — there's no verb

أنا تعبان *ana taʿbān* — I (am) tired. هي بالبيت *hi bil-bēt* — she (is) at home.
Past: كنت *kint* — I was ⚠ (some say *kunt*).

### 5.4 Present tense — one verb, all people

راح / يروح — to go

| | Arabic | Say |
|---|---|---|
| I go | أروح | arūḥ |
| you go (m) | تروح | tirūḥ |
| **you go (f)** | **تروحين** | **tirūḥīn** |
| he goes | يروح | yirūḥ |
| she goes | تروح | tirūḥ |
| we go | نروح | nirūḥ |
| you all go | تروحون | tirūḥūn |
| they go | يروحون | yirūḥūn |

Pattern: **a- / ti- / ti-…-īn / yi- / ti- / ni- / ti-…-ūn / yi-…-ūn**. Every verb works like this.

### 5.5 Past tense

| | Arabic | Say |
|---|---|---|
| I went | رحت | riḥt |
| you went (m) | رحت | riḥt |
| **you went (f)** | **رحتي** | **riḥti** |
| he went | راح | rāḥ |
| she went | راحت | rāḥat |
| we went | رحنا | riḥna |
| you all went | رحتو | riḥtu |
| they went | راحوا | rāḥaw ⚠ |

Verbs ending in a vowel take **-ēt**: سويت *sawwēt* (I did), صحيت *ṣiḥēt* (I woke up), لقيت *ligēt* (I found).

### 5.6 Want, will, am doing

| Pattern | Example | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| أبي + verb | أبي أنام *abi anām* | I want to sleep |
| تبين + verb (to her) | تبين تاكلين؟ *tibīn tākilīn?* | do you want to eat? |
| ودي + verb | ودي أشوفك *widdi ashūfik* | I'd love to see you |
| ب + verb = future | بنام *banām* | I'm going to sleep |
| قاعد + verb = right now | قاعد أشتغل *gāʿid ashtaghil* | I'm working (right now) |
| قاعدة + verb (her) | قاعدة تطبخ *gāʿda tiṭbakh* | she's cooking |

Also heard: أبغى *abgha* = I want (same meaning, said all over Saudi, especially in the west).

### 5.7 Saying no

| Pattern | Example | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| ما + verb | ما أدري *ma adri* → مدري *madri* | I don't know |
| ما + verb | ما فهمت *ma fhimt* | I didn't understand |
| مو + adjective/noun | مو زين *mū zēn* | not good |
| ماني + adjective | ماني فاهم *māni fāhim* | I'm not getting it |
| لا + verb = don't! | لا تزعلين *la tizʿalīn* | don't be upset (to her) |
| ما فيه | ما فيه مشكلة *ma fīh mushkila* | there's no problem |

Riyadh also says مب *mub* (and مهوب *muhub*) for مو — recognize it.

### 5.8 Have, there is

عندي *ʿindi* — I have. عندك *ʿindik* — you have. ما عندي *ma ʿindi* — I don't have.
فيه *fīh* — there is. ما فيه *ma fīh* — there isn't.

### 5.9 Time words that change meaning

| Word | Say | Example |
|---|---|---|
| توّ + ending | taww- | توني صحيت *tawwni ṣiḥēt* — I just woke up |
| بعد | baʿad | still / also: وأنا بعد *w ana baʿad* — me too |
| ما بعد | ma baʿad | not yet: ما بعد أكلت *ma baʿad akalt* — I haven't eaten yet |
| عاد | ʿād | then / anymore (you'll hear it constantly as a filler) |

### Phase 3 — Printables (A4)

Everything prints on **A4**. Each printable is an HTML page with a print stylesheet, and a script (`npm run pdf`) renders them to PDF with headless Chromium (Playwright `page.pdf()`), saved to `print/pdf/`.

- **Self-host the Arabic font** (Noto Naskh Arabic, open license) inside the project so PDFs look right everywhere. Don't rely on Google Fonts for printing.
- Arabic at **28pt minimum** on practice sheets. Rows start at the **right** margin.

Printables to build:

1. **Letter tracing sheets** — one page per shape group from the alphabet page. Each letter in all its forms: one solid example, then light-grey copies to trace, then empty guideline rows. Show the baseline; letters like ر و ي ن drop below it.
2. **Word writing sheets** — per stage topic: Arabic word, say, meaning, three grey tracing copies, blank lines.
3. **Fold-and-test lists** — Arabic on the right half, meaning on the left; fold the page down the middle to test yourself.
4. **Phrase cards** — 8 cards per A4 with cut marks. Front: Arabic. Back: say + meaning, laid out mirrored so double-sided printing lines up.
5. **Weekly tracker** — 7 days: minutes studied, new words, voice notes exchanged, tutor session, one sentence I said today.

## Part 10 — Sources and further reading

- **Najdi Arabic** overview — phonology (ق = g, merged ض/ظ, affricates fading among younger speakers), negation with *ma*/*la*, time particles (*taww*, *baʿad*, *ʿād*), progressive *gāʿid*: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Najdi_Arabic
- **Alajmi, N. (2023).** Regional and Sociolinguistic Variation of Personal Pronouns in Dialects of Najdi Arabic. *JLTR* 14(5). Source for the pronoun table and for *-ik* being the standard 2nd-person feminine ending in Najdi: https://jltr.academypublication.com/index.php/jltr/article/view/6639
- **Binturki, T.** The Acquisition of Negation in Najdi Arabic (verbal *la*/*ma*, non-verbal *muhub*): https://kuscholarworks.ku.edu
- **Al-Rojaie, Y. (2021).** The pragmatic functions of religious expressions in Najdi Arabic — why *in shāʾ allah*, *mā shāʾ allah* etc. appear everywhere in casual speech: https://doi.org/10.1108/SJLS-03-2021-0006
- **Riyadh future prefix ب-** (vs. راح elsewhere) — native-speaker discussion: https://forum.wordreference.com/threads/saudi-arabic-%D8%A3%D8%A8%D9%8A-%D8%A3%D8%A8%D8%BA%D9%89.2416319/
- **Mo3jam — Najdi dictionary** (crowd-sourced; useful for checking words): https://en.mo3jam.com/dialect/Najdi
- **Ingham, B. (1994).** *Najdi Arabic: Central Arabian.* John Benjamins. The standard academic grammar — for the tutor, not for you yet.
- **Saudi, not "Najdi":** Saudis call their everyday speech سعودي. The Najdi sources above describe the central speech that modern Saudi grew from — still the best written descriptions of it.
- **Listening beyond her voice notes:** Saudis post huge amounts of daily-life vlogs on Snapchat and TikTok — short, casual, real speech. Better fit than films for this plan. Also: the فنجان (Fnjan) podcast by Thmanyah on YouTube — long, relaxed conversations in natural Saudi; and the Saudi cartoon comedy Masameer County (مسامير) on Netflix.
